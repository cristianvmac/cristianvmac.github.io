Remember when Google Business Profile, back when it was still called Google My Business, first became something every local business had to have? One day it was optional. A few years later, not having one meant being invisible on Google Maps.

OpenAI just had that moment, and it did it quietly.

Businesses can now claim a profile in ChatGPT, update their information and connect a product feed. Eligible US ecommerce and online-service companies can go a step further and deploy a branded Business Agent that talks to customers on their behalf.

And this isn't a niche experiment. ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion weekly users.

So, is ChatGPT becoming the new Google Business Profile? Here are eight things I think every small business owner should be thinking about.

## 1. Your online presence matters more than ever

A nice website on its own isn't enough anymore. Your business needs to be understood everywhere customers search, compare and make decisions.

That used to mean Google, social media and review sites. Now it also includes AI assistants. Each one is a place where a customer could find you, or find your competitor.

## 2. AI needs to understand what you actually do

When someone asks ChatGPT for a recommendation, it has to answer some basic questions about your business:

- Who are your customers?
- What services do you offer?
- Where do you operate?
- What makes you different?

If your information is incomplete or inconsistent, how can AI confidently recommend you?

Your website says one thing, your Google Business Profile says another, and a directory listing from five years ago says something else again. A person might work out what's going on. An AI system is far more likely to just recommend someone who is clearer.

## 3. Your reputation matters

Reviews, customer experiences and everything else people can find about your business shape how you're perceived, by customers and by AI tools.

There's no shortcut here. A reputation builds slowly, one happy customer and one honest review at a time. That's exactly why it's worth starting now rather than when you suddenly need it.

## 4. Product information is becoming critical

For businesses that sell online, product data is turning into a visibility tool in its own right. Accurate descriptions, up-to-date pricing and real-time availability can make a big difference to how customers discover and evaluate your products.

With ChatGPT now accepting product feeds, a vague description or an out-of-date price isn't just a small annoyance anymore. It could be the reason your product never comes up.

## 5. Your website isn't disappearing. Its role is changing.

Some people will read news like this and decide websites are finished. I don't think so.

Someone might discover you through ChatGPT, but before they buy, book or call, they'll still want to visit your website. They'll want to understand your services, see your work and decide whether they trust you.

Your website is moving from being the place people find you to being the place they confirm you. That makes it more important, not less. It's also one of the main sources AI tools rely on to understand your business in the first place.

## 6. We need to rethink visibility

Ranking on Google still matters. As we covered in [Why Your Small Business Needs an Online Presence](/blog/why-your-small-business-needs-an-online-presence/), Google is still where most local searches begin.

But what happens when customers start asking ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity for recommendations instead?

Would your business appear?

For most small businesses, the honest answer right now is "I have no idea." And that's the problem.

## 7. Small businesses need to pay attention now

Today, OpenAI's Business Agents are limited to eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses. The rollout for local businesses is still evolving.

So you could wait. But I wouldn't wait until customers have completely changed their habits before thinking about this.

The businesses that did well with Google Business Profile were the ones that set it up early, kept it accurate and collected reviews before everyone else caught on. The same thing is likely to happen here.

## 8. Nobody has figured out the full picture yet

I want to be honest: there are still more questions than answers.

- How do you measure AI visibility?
- How do you know why one business gets recommended over another?
- How do you turn that visibility into actual customers?

Nobody has fully cracked these yet, not agencies, not marketers and not the platforms themselves. Anyone who claims to have a guaranteed formula for "ranking in ChatGPT" is guessing.

## What you can do today

You don't need to understand every detail of AI search to get ready for it. The fundamentals are the same ones that have always worked:

- **Make your website clear.** Say plainly what you do, who you serve and where you operate.
- **Keep your information consistent.** Same name, address, phone number, hours and services everywhere.
- **Keep your Google Business Profile complete and active.** It's still one of the most trusted sources of business information online.
- **Build your reviews.** Ask happy customers, and reply to every review you get.
- **Keep product data accurate.** If you sell online, make sure descriptions, prices and stock levels are always up to date.

## Easy to find, easy to understand, easy to trust

Your business needs to be easy to find, easy to understand and easy to trust. Not just by people, but more and more by AI.

I think we're entering an interesting new chapter for small business. The ones who get their foundations right now will be in the best position, whichever platform customers end up asking.
